# The farm pack

Thirty-six models for building a race map that runs through a working farm: buildings,
fencing, yard clutter, vehicles, implements and livestock. Everything is generated by
`Tools/blender/models/farm_*.py` on the same principle as the rest of this project's
geometry — a script you can re-run and diff, not a binary you can only replace.

## Getting it into Unity

```powershell
.\Tools\blender\build-models.ps1
```

Then, with the Editor focused so it has imported the new FBX files:

```
Tools > Toebeans > Farm > Set Up Generated Farm Assets
```

That creates the shared materials, points every model's importer at them, bakes the duck
quacks, and writes a prefab per model into `Assets/Prefabs/Farm`. It is idempotent — run
it again after a re-export and it repairs whatever drifted. Material *colours* are only
stamped when a material is first created, so a colour you tune by hand in the inspector
survives a re-run.

There is no list of model names in the C#. The Blender build writes a manifest describing
what it made — names, sizes, palettes, part hierarchies, how each thing should collide —
and the setup tool reads that. **Adding a prop to the pack needs no C# change at all.**

## The three things worth knowing before you lay a track

### The barns are drivable through

`Farm_Barn` has a 4.2 × 4.0 m opening through **both** gable ends with nothing between
them — no threshold, no floor slab, no tie beam below the wall plate. A kart is 1.24 m
across its front track, so that is three abreast with room. `Farm_BarnSmall` is the same
idea at 3.6 × 3.4 m, and `Farm_Shed` is open-fronted across all three bays.

Run the track straight through. Everything about those buildings is shaped to protect it:
the concrete plinth stops short of the doorways rather than running past as a kerb, and
the sliding doors are modelled parked open against the *outside* of the wall.

### Fencing belongs to `BarrierLine`, not to you

The six fence sections are authored as barrier sections, not as props. Put them on a
`BarrierLine` and you get spacing, corner fitting, and one welded zero-friction wall swept
down the whole run — so a kart drifting along a fence slides instead of snagging on each
post.

They therefore carry **no colliders**, deliberately. A `BarrierLine` with *Build Blocking
Wall* off has no collision whatsoever; that switch is the collision, not the prefabs.
Adding colliders back to the sections turns a curve into a saw of protruding corners,
which is the exact thing that system exists to avoid.

Set *Facing* to **Along Path** and *Corner Fit* to **Fit Ends** for a run of fence.
`Farm_FenceGate` is swung back along the line rather than across it, so a gateway is
drivable.

### Collision is chosen per prop, and it is in the manifest

- **Mesh** — buildings, troughs, the pump, the implements. The mesh is the collider, same
  as everything else this project generates.
- **Box** — bales, crates, barrels, the log pile. A mesh collider on a bale buys nothing
  at kart speed and costs a broadphase entry per instance.
- **Capsule** — the animals, fitted to the **body** only, not to the whole animal
  including its head, tail and the swing of its legs. They also get a kinematic
  `Rigidbody`, which is what makes a moving collider cheap.
- **None** — the fence sections and the scarecrow.

## The animals

Every animal is a rigid puppet — a body with its head, jaw, legs, tail, ears and wings
hung off it as separate meshes, each pivoted on the joint it actually turns about. Nothing
is skinned and nothing carries a baked clip.

`FarmAnimal` walks and grazes them by posing those parts directly. The legs swing off
**distance travelled**, not off a clock, so a stride is always one stride of ground at any
speed and the feet cannot skate. Forty of them are forty transform writes a frame.

Useful knobs on the component: `roamRadius`, `walkSpeed`, `waypoints` and `pauseSeconds`
for where it goes, `strideLength` and `legSwing` for how it looks getting there. Set
`habit` to **Stand** for an animal in a pen. Select one in the scene to see its route
drawn as a gizmo.

If you would rather key an animation by hand, nothing stops you: every joint is a real
GameObject with a sensible pivot, which is exactly what a clip needs to address.

### Ducks on a pond

Drop `Farm_PondDuck` on the water and set **Water Level** to the pond surface's Y. It
floats at the right depth (the duck sinks by a `waterline` measured off the model, not
guessed), drifts in a slow loop, paddles, preens — and quacks when a kart goes past.

The quack triggers on something with a non-kinematic `Rigidbody` coming within
`hearingRadius` and actually moving, above `minPasserSpeed`; a kart parked alongside does
not set it off. `reactChance` is below 1 on purpose, so a raft of ducks answers raggedly
rather than in unison. The bill opens while the clip plays.

The four quacks are synthesised, not sourced — see `Editor/FarmQuackBaker.cs`, which
explains what actually makes a buzzy sawtooth sound like a duck. Retune and re-bake with
`Tools > Toebeans > Farm > Bake Duck Quacks`.

## The vehicles

`FarmVehicle` turns the wheels off the distance the vehicle has moved and points the front
pair where it is going. That means it is correct whether the vehicle is driven by a
script, dragged around in the editor, or towed — park the hay wagon behind the tractor,
move the tractor, and the wagon's wheels turn because the wagon moved. The tractor's
steering wheel turns with them.

`FarmWindpump` spins the rotor and yaws the head to face `windHeading`. Point every
windpump on a map at the same heading and the whole map agrees about the wind.

## Multiplayer

The farm is built to the project's multiplayer-first constraint. A cow's position, a
duck's bob and a windmill's gust are all pure functions of `(seed, time)` — nothing
accumulates, so nothing drifts and there is no animation state to put on the wire. Seeds
default to a hash of the placed position, which is identical on every client.

That only pays off once "time" means the same thing everywhere. One line, from wherever
the netcode ends up living:

```csharp
FarmClock.Source = () => NetworkManager.ServerTime.Time;
```

Until then it runs off local time, which is right for single player and the editor. The
animals carry colliders, so this is worth doing before the farm map ships.

## Looking at the models without opening Blender

```powershell
& $env:BLENDER_PATH --background --factory-startup --python .\Tools\blender\preview.py -- Farm_Tractor
& $env:BLENDER_PATH --background --factory-startup --python .\Tools\blender\contact_sheet.py -- farm_props
```

The first renders one prop on a one-metre floor grid; the second lays a whole manifest out
on a grid in one picture, which is the only reliable way to catch a palette drifting one
prop at a time.

## What still needs a human eye

The pack has been checked in Blender and compile-checked against Unity 6, but it has not
been driven through. Three things to look at the first time a kart is in it:

- **The barn doorways at speed.** They are wide enough on paper. Whether the approach
  reads as an opening or as a wall is a question only driving it answers.
- **Animal capsules against a kart.** The capsule is fitted to the body, so a cow's head
  and tail are not solid. That is deliberate, and it may still feel wrong to clip through
  a head.
- **Quack loudness.** `minDistance` 4 m and `maxDistance` 34 m are a guess at how far a
  duck should carry over engine noise.
